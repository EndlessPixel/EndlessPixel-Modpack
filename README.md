# EndlessPixel Modpack

# About EndlessPixel

EndlessPixel is a comprehensive Minecraft modpack designed to elevate your gameplay experience. It integrates a diverse collection of mods, resource packs, and shader packs, catering to different player needs—whether you seek utility enhancements, performance optimization, or immersive visual upgrades, EndlessPixel has you covered.

Adhering to the core philosophy of "Stability First, Experience Supreme", each version of the modpack undergoes multiple rounds of stress testing. We strive to avoid common issues such as mod conflicts and game crashes, allowing players to enjoy the complete modded gaming experience directly without spending a lot of time debugging configurations.

## 2. Optimized Performance for Smooth Gameplay

We have meticulously selected and optimized performance-focused mods (such as Sodium, Lithium, and Starlight) to significantly reduce frame drops and lag. The modpack is compatible with both low and mid-range devices, ensuring a smooth gaming experience even in complex modded environments with rich textures and dynamic effects.

## 3. Immersive Visual Experience

The modpack comes with high-quality resource packs and shaders (based on the Sodium + Iris rendering core) that enhance the game's visual appeal without compromising performance. Players can switch between different visual styles in the in-game settings to create a personalized immersive gaming atmosphere—from realistic photorealistic effects to cute cartoon-style textures.

## 4. Regular Updates for Long-Term Playability

We provide frequent updates to keep the modpack fresh and compatible. Updates include integrating the latest mod versions, fixing known bugs, optimizing configurations, and adapting to new Minecraft official versions. Detailed update logs are released with each version, allowing players to clearly understand the content of changes.

## Runtime Environment

- Client: Optional installation (fully functional as a client-side-only modpack)

EndlessPixel works seamlessly as a client-side-only modpack—no server installation is required. However, installing it on a server unlocks all features. Some advanced functionalities may not work properly on vanilla servers, but you can still play normally without server-side installation (you will only miss out on certain advanced features).

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

## 1. Q: Why does the series start from v5 instead of v1?
A: The numbering of this modpack series starts at v5 because it builds on several earlier, non-public development versions that preceded the official launch of the 1.21.4 branch. Specifically, the initial iterations of the modpack were tied to older Minecraft versions: we released **1.20.1-v1**, **1.20.6-v2**, and **1.21.1-v3** as internal test builds to refine core mechanics and mod compatibility.
Following these versions, we developed **1.21.4-v4** as a pilot build for the then-new 1.21.4 Minecraft update. However, this v4 build suffered from severe mod conflicts, disorganized configuration files, and inconsistent gameplay balance—issues that made it unfit for public release. To address these problems comprehensively, we decided to wipe the slate clean and conduct a full restructure of the modpack: we overhauled mod selection, optimized dependency chains, rebalanced gameplay parameters, and fixed underlying compatibility bugs from scratch.
The result of this complete rebuild was **1.21.4-v5**, which marked the first stable, polished version suitable for public distribution. That is why v5 is recognized as the official starting point of the current modpack series.

## 2. Q: How long will it take to update the modpack after Mojang releases a new Minecraft version?

A: Beta versions of the modpack are usually rolled out **within a week** following the launch of a new official Minecraft version. In optimal scenarios—such as when the new Minecraft update focuses on minor bug fixes or content tweaks instead of overhauls to core game mechanics—the beta version may even be released **on the same day or the very next day** after Mojang’s update. The release timeline for the **stable official version** of the modpack, however, is largely dependent on the update speed of major mod developers. Many core mods (e.g., those responsible for world generation, gameplay mechanics, or performance optimization) need to be adapted to the new Minecraft version’s codebase to avoid crashes or compatibility issues. We will only push the official modpack update once all critical mods have been updated and thoroughly tested for stability.

## 3. Q: Why is the number of mods uneven across different series of the modpack?

A: The discrepancy in mod counts across different modpack series stems from the fact that each series is tailored to a distinct main Minecraft version. Mod development is a community-driven effort, and major mod developers may not always update their creations in lockstep with Mojang’s official version releases. For newer Minecraft versions, some mods may be temporarily unavailable due to ongoing adaptation work, while for older versions, a larger pool of fully compatible mods often exists. To ensure the modpack runs smoothly without crashes or conflicts, we have to make two key adjustments: either **abandon mods that are incompatible** with the target Minecraft version, or **adopt corresponding alternative ported versions** (when available) that fill the functional gap left by missing mods. These necessary trade-offs directly lead to the variation in the total number of mods across different series.

## 4. Q: Can OptiFine be added to the modpack?

A: This modpack uses **Sodium + Iris Shaders** as its default rendering core—a combination that is widely recognized for its superior performance optimization and modern shader support. Unfortunately, this setup is **fully incompatible with OptiFine**; forcing OptiFine into the modpack will trigger immediate game crashes or severe rendering glitches upon launch. If you want to use visual shaders to enhance the game’s graphics, there is no need to switch back to OptiFine at all—simply place your preferred shader files into the **shaderpacks** folder in the modpack directory, and they will be detectable and usable via the Iris in-game settings. If you insist on using OptiFine despite these limitations, you will need to manually delete all rendering-related mods from the pack first (including Sodium, Iris, and any other mods that modify the game’s rendering pipeline), then install OptiFine separately. Please note that this manual modification may cause other unexpected compatibility issues.

## 5. Q: Will a NeoForge version of the modpack be released in the future?

A: At present, **approximately 80% of the core mods** that define this modpack’s gameplay experience only maintain active Fabric branches, with no plans for official NeoForge ports. As a result, we have no concrete migration plan for a NeoForge version in the short term. Moving forward, if the NeoForge ecosystem matures to the point where all key mods (including those for core mechanics, performance tuning, and content expansion) have stable, fully functional ports, we will consider developing both Fabric and NeoForge versions of the modpack in parallel. That said, the existing Fabric version will remain our mainline focus, as it offers better stability and a more robust modding ecosystem for our target gameplay. Additionally, NeoForge currently lags behind Fabric in terms of optimization performance in our internal tests, and it has poor compatibility with many niche but popular mods in our pack. These factors mean that a NeoForge version may not even be on our development radar, depending on how the modding landscape evolves.

## 6. Q: Why do "old version" mods still remain in the new modpack releases?
A: The retention of older-version mods in new modpack releases is a deliberate, test-verified decision. In some cases, mod developers have not yet released updated versions that match the main Minecraft version of the current modpack. Instead of removing these mods entirely (which would strip away key gameplay features or quality-of-life functions), we conduct rigorous compatibility testing to check if the older mod versions can run on the new Minecraft version. If these legacy mods are confirmed to work **without causing crashes, game-breaking bugs, or conflicts with other core mods**, we will temporarily retain them in the pack. Once the official updated versions of these mods are released by their developers, we will immediately replace the old versions in the next modpack update. Moreover, we will explicitly mark the phrase **"complete migration"** in the update log to inform users that all mods in the pack are now updated to the latest compatible versions.

## 7. Q: After a bug is reported by the community, how long will it take to be fixed?

A: Once a bug is reported by the community, our team follows a standardized troubleshooting process to ensure efficient and targeted resolution, with the timeline varying based on the root cause of the issue. First, we will collect detailed information provided by the reporter—including crash logs, step-by-step reproduction methods, and the specific modpack version in use—then conduct internal verification to confirm whether the bug can be stably reproduced in our test environment. Only after confirming the reproducibility will we proceed to the next stage of root cause analysis.
- **Modpack-specific issue**: If the bug is determined to be caused by our modpack configuration—such as mod conflicts, incorrect dependency settings, or custom script errors—our team will prioritize the fix and aim to roll out a hotfix or patch update within **1–2 weeks**. For critical bugs that cause game crashes, unplayable gameplay states, or data loss, we will expedite the process and push an emergency fix within 3–5 business days whenever possible.
- **Upstream-related issue**: If the bug is traced to upstream factors outside our control—including native Minecraft bugs, Java runtime environment glitches, Windows/Linux/macOS system compatibility problems, graphics driver issues, or bugs in the original mods included in the pack—we cannot directly resolve it ourselves. In such cases, we will either open a detailed issue ticket with the relevant upstream developers (e.g., the Minecraft development team, mod authors, or driver providers) or attach our verification data to existing related tickets. We will then monitor the progress of the official fix closely, and once an official patch is released, we will promptly integrate it into the modpack, re-test the issue to confirm resolution, and finally mark the community bug report as closed.
